Sanskrit Language

Introduction

Sanskrit is one of the oldest and most important classical languages of the Indian subcontinent. It has played a major role in Indian literature, philosophy, religion, science, mathematics, medicine, and education. Sanskrit has a very rich literary and intellectual tradition that has continued for more than two thousand years.

Ramayana

Written by Walmiki in sanskrit

The Ramayana is one of the great Sanskrit epics traditionally attributed to Valmiki. It tells the story of Rama, Sita, Lakshmana, Hanuman, and other important characters and has had a major influence on Indian literature and culture.
And The Mahabharata is one of the largest epic works in world literature. It tells the story of the Kuru dynasty and the conflict between the Pandavas and Kauravas. The Bhagavad Gita is an important section of the Mahabharata. Kalidasa is one of the most famous authors in classical Sanskrit literature. His works include Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsha, and Kumarasambhavam. His writing is known for its poetic beauty and descriptions of nature and human emotions.

Sanskrit in Modern India

Sanskrit continues to be studied in India in schools, universities, traditional institutions, research centers, and cultural organizations. It is also used in religious ceremonies, classical studies, literature, and modern academic research. The phrase ‘World Sanskrit Day’ elucidates the connotation of Sanskrit holding a substantial meaning at the global level, ubiquitous to the whole world, which is beyond the identity of being one of the languages of the Indian Subcontinent. Thus, it is important to explore the narrative that the Sanskrit language has around itself, to analyze its various avenues and evolution from the past and present, and to envision the contemporary relevance it has. Sanskrit belongs to the Satem group of Indo-European languages of South Asia. Besides being an ancient classical language, it is also a liturgical language of Hinduism and Buddhism primarily, and occasionally used in Jainism. It is one of the twenty-two official languages of India and an ancestor of the modern Indo-Aryan languages. Its status in the cultures of South and Southeast Asia is similar to that of Latin and Greek in Europe and it has evolved into and influenced, many modern languages of the world.
